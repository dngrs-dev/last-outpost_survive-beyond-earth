# Mission balance and reproducible simulations

Run from `backend/`:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe balance_missions.py --runs 200 --seed-start 30000 --output ../docs/mission-balance.json
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe balance_missions.py --runs 50 --seed-start 40000 --stations oceanus taurus --crew alex lin
```

The committed JSON is generated by this command. Do not edit its numbers. Its model digest covers definitions and simulation rules; a test detects stale reports. There are no network requests, NASA API calls, extra dependencies, or frontend automation in the benchmark.

## What success means

**Operational success** is reaching relief with `successful_handover` or `perfect_handover`. An emergency handover counts as survival, but does not meet the operational reserve/system standards. Science remains independent of the survival ending.

The existing stars are preserved: zero if nobody survives; otherwise one for any survivor, another for the whole initial crew, and another for completing all science objectives. Three stars do not imply a perfect operational handover. The report therefore includes endings, any survival, full-crew survival, science completion and the entire star distribution separately.

## Policies and uncertainty

Both policies use the same capacity-checked, mission-specific recommended cargo and Alex, Maya, Lin and Sam. These are starting suggestions for four crew, not optimal loadouts or mandatory quantities. Different teams and cargo change the result.

- **Basic:** normal rations while affordable, explicit emergency rations when depleted, first affordable eligible incident response, best eligible lead, legal support crew, no planned assignments.
- **Careful:** scores visible expected incident effects and costs, books maintenance and research independently, treats medical conditions while reserving caregiver and patient, progresses seismic projects, and rests free crew. Uses present state and action previews, never future incidents, random outcomes, or seed inspection. It pursues science, so it can trade survival margin for science and is not an optimal solver.

Pilot runs and tuning used seeds 0-199, 10000-10199 and 20000-20199. Definitions were frozen before the final validation range 30000-30199. All 6 missions and both policies are simulated: **2,400 final validation campaigns**. Wilson 95% intervals describe variation across seeded simulated outcomes under these policies. They are not estimates of children's performance. Small deterministic tests also cover different team sizes and specialists, legal complete campaigns, no hidden seed access, and policy improvement on extreme.

The user's approximate 95% easy / 5% extreme anchors are treated as forgiving easy play (at least 90%, preferably 95%+) and about 1-10% routine operational success on extreme, with substantially better chances through careful choices. We do not force an arbitrary share of easy missions to fail. Full scientific success is an additional challenge. Future playtests with children aged 7-12 must calibrate actual difficulty and comprehension.

## Authored tuning

Only fictional equipment and objective balancing values changed. NASA lunar coordinates, weekly daylight averaging and physical lunar-day period stay intact.

- Standard outposts have less excess solar generation, making energy preparation meaningful rather than almost automatic. Air filters begin at 85% condition.
- Descartes has two additional water units, reducing a sharp end-of-mission reserve cliff.
- Taurus gains modest solar and oxygen output, so oxygen production at its starting condition can cover four crew in good power conditions. The extreme science target includes three seismic projects rather than two; three independent five-week projects fit inside the eighteen-week mission without depending on lucky event credit. Incident science remains an alternative.
- Launch minima and recommended cargo follow the revised targets. Requirements fund one attempt per unit; failed work, repair needs, deprivation and power shortages remain genuine risks.

## Research used

[Pre-registered difficulty/skill study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9890114/) cautions against equating a particular difficulty balance with enjoyment. [Statistical Modelling of Level Difficulty in Puzzle Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03305) supports examining multiple dimensions of difficulty rather than only a binary result. [NIST's proportion interval guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) provides the Wilson interval method. The numerical targets here are game-design choices, not findings or NASA safety limits.

## Frozen validation results

| Outpost | Basic operational success | Careful operational success | Careful three stars |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| oceanus | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| alpha | 91.5% | 95.0% | 80.5% |
| fra_mauro | 89.0% | 98.5% | 87.0% |
| hadley | 70.0% | 100.0% | 58.0% |
| descartes | 19.0% | 99.5% | 19.5% |
| taurus | 3.0% | 84.0% | 16.5% |

Easy basic success was 200/200 (Wilson 95% interval 98.1-100%); extreme basic was 6/200 (1.4-6.4%). We retained the very forgiving easy mission instead of manufacturing losses. Extreme careful play reached operational relief in 168/200 runs, while only 33/200 achieved all three stars. This leaves a measurable reward for thoughtful planning and an additional science challenge.
